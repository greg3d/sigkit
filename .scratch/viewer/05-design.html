What this draft proposes

  1. Sidebar on the left, about 260 px wide, always visible. It lists recording → stream → channel. A dot shows each channel's palette colour: filled when the channel is on a panel, hollow when not. A count shows how many panels it is on.
  2. The fit readout and the fit toggle move into the tree. They sit under each timestamped stream and on its residual row. The bottom bar's channel picker goes away.
  3. One time view for all panels, each panel with its own value view. Panels sit side by side in vertical mode and stacked in horizontal mode. Only the first panel draws time labels (the last one in horizontal mode). Every panel draws its own value labels.
  4. Drop targets: a dashed outline in the channel's colour over a panel means overlay. A bar on a panel edge (within 22 px) means new panel. The tree tints red when a legend is dragged onto it, meaning remove. A panel disappears with its last channel.
  5. Legend chips are the drag handle for channels already on a panel: drag one to another panel to move it, or to a panel edge to split it out.
  6. Cursor: one line across every panel, with a dot on each channel's nearest sample. Each legend chip gains a second line showing the exact value and unit, then the raw stamp in clock units for timestamped streams. The bottom bar shows t, plus UTC for each recording that has an anchor.
  7. Dropping a second recording adds it to the tree. It also opens a new panel with the channel matching the first panel's first channel, which is how the pair preset ends up side by side.
  8. A channel can be on several panels, but only once per panel. That is what makes "overlaid here, alone there" in the acceptance criteria possible.

Open questions

  • Palette with two recordings. The glossary colours channels "by their place in the recording", so pair-a accel/z and pair-b accel/z would get the same colour on an overlay. The draft numbers colours across all open recordings in tree order instead.
  • Mixed units on one panel, such as m/s² over a ms residual. The draft allows it, and the value labels drop the unit when the units differ.
  • Autoscale scope. A would fit the panel under the pointer and ⇧A every panel. Alternatively A fits every panel.
  • Readout precision. The draft prints a value at the precision of its column's dtype after gain and offset: 7 significant digits for f32, 15 for f64, and the integer times gain for integer types.
  • Sidebar width: fixed, or resizable with a drag handle?
